# 🇺🇸 English: Working with Interpreters & Communicating with LEP Individuals

## 1. Executive Summary & The Federal Standard

Under federal law (Title VI, Executive Order 13166, and Section 1557 of the Affordable Care Act), federally funded entities, healthcare facilities, housing authorities, and public sector organizations are legally required to provide **meaningful access** to Limited English Proficient (LEP) individuals.

Relying on ad-hoc translation, bilingual staff pulled from unrelated duties, or untrained bystanders introduces severe legal exposure, diagnostic error, and civil rights violations. Whether you are using **Abrevia’s automated bilingual relay**, our **on-demand certified human interpreter (OPI/VRI) gateway**, or an in-person interpreter, adhering to standardized professional protocols is essential.

---

## 2. Core Protocol: How to Work Effectively with an Interpreter

### Phase 1: Pre-Session Preparation & Briefing
* **Brief the Interpreter (30–60 seconds):** Before connecting with the constituent, provide the interpreter with the context: the topic of the session (e.g., medical intake, lease renewal, delivery logistics, legal notice), specialized terminology that may arise, and the constituent's country or dialect if known.
* **Confirm Language & Dialect:** Confirm that the interpreter matches the constituent's language and regional dialect (e.g., Mexican Spanish vs. Caribbean vs. Central American).
* **Provide Documents in Advance:** If forms, discharge instructions, or leases will be reviewed, have them open or shared beforehand.

### Phase 2: Positioning & Setting Up the Space
* **In-Person Positioning:** Place the interpreter **next to and slightly behind the LEP constituent**, or in a triangular setup. This ensures your direct visual line of sight and body language remain focused on the constituent—not on the interpreter.
* **Telephonic / Remote Video (OPI / VRI):** Use a high-quality headset or secure speakerphone in a private, quiet room. Ensure background noise is eliminated to protect privacy (HIPAA) and acoustic clarity.

### Phase 3: Communication Rules During the Interaction
1. **Always Speak Directly in the First Person:**  
   * ✅ *Correct:* *"What symptoms are you experiencing today?"* or *"When can our technician enter your apartment?"*  
   * ❌ *Incorrect:* *"Ask him what his symptoms are."* or *"Tell her she needs to sign here."*
2. **Chunk Your Delivery (1 to 2 Sentences):** Speak in short, digestible segments. Pause every 1–2 sentences to allow consecutive interpretation without overloading the interpreter's working memory.
3. **Use Plain Language:** Eliminate jargon, technical acronyms, bureaucratic phrasing, and cultural idioms (*"ballpark figure"*, *"beat around the bush"*). Plain English translates with 99%+ higher fidelity.
4. **Maintain Natural Eye Contact:** Look directly at the LEP constituent while speaking and while they respond, even though you are hearing the interpreter's voice.
5. **Everything Spoken is Interpreted:** Professional certified interpreters operate under a strict code of ethics requiring them to interpret **everything** said in the room verbatim. Never hold "side conversations" with the interpreter.
6. **Verify Understanding (The Teach-Back Method):** Instead of asking *"Do you understand?"* (which constituents often answer *"Yes"* out of politeness or shame), ask: *"To make sure I explained everything clearly, could you tell me in your own words what our next steps are?"*

### Phase 4: Critical DOs and DON'Ts

| ✅ WHAT TO DO | ❌ WHAT NEVER TO DO |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Speak in the first person** directly to the constituent. | **Never address the interpreter** in third person (*"Tell her..."*). |
| **Pause frequently** every 1–2 sentences. | **Never speak in lengthy paragraphs** without pausing. |
| **Use plain, clear English** and short words. | **Never use idioms, slang, legalisms, or acronyms**. |
| **Use qualified or certified interpreters**. | **NEVER use minor children or family members** to interpret. |
| **Address cultural nuances respectfully**. | **Never raise your volume** (LEP is a language difference, not a hearing impairment). |
| **Encourage the interpreter to request clarification** if needed. | **Never hold private side conversations** with the interpreter in front of the client. |

## 1. Estándar Regulatorio Federal

Bajo el **Título VI de la Ley de Derechos Civiles de 1964**, la **Orden Ejecutiva 13166** y la **Sección 1557 de la Ley de Cuidado de Salud a Bajo Precio (ACA)**, no proporcionar acceso lingüístico oportuno y significativo a personas con Dominio Limitado del Inglés (LEP) constituye discriminación ilegal por origen nacional.

## 2. Protocolo de Comunicación Eficaz

1. **Hable en primera persona:** Diríjase siempre al usuario, no al intérprete (*"¿En qué podemos ayudarle hoy?"* en lugar de *"Pregúntele en qué le ayudo"*).
2. **Haga pausas cortas:** Formule una o dos oraciones y pause para permitir que la interpretación sea exacta y fluida.
3. **Use lenguaje sencillo:** Evite modismos, tecnicismos o jerga que dificulte la traducción.
4. **Todo se interpreta:** El código de ética profesional obliga al intérprete a traducir absolutamente todo lo que se dice en la sala o en la llamada.
5. **Nunca utilice a menores de edad o familiares:** Para trámites médicos, legales, contractuales o de vivienda, el uso de menores de edad como intérpretes está estrictamente prohibido por normas federales de derechos civiles.
