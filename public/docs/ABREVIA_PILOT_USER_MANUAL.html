🏛️ 1. Executive Overview & The Problem Abrevia Solves

Across healthcare outreach, residential property management, logistics dispatch, municipal public works, and field services, English-speaking operators interact daily with Limited English Proficient (LEP) Spanish-speaking constituents. 🗣️ 3. Federal Compliance & Best Practices Job Aid

Under Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, Executive Order 13166, and Section 1557 of the Affordable Care Act, federally funded programs, healthcare clinics, and housing authorities are legally required to provide competent, free language access. When working with interpreters or sending translated notices, adhere to these professional standards:

✅ WHAT TO DO (Mandated Best Practices) ❌ WHAT NEVER TO DO (Civil Rights Liabilities)
Speak in the first person directly to the constituent (*"What symptoms are you feeling?"*). Never speak in the third person (*"Tell him..."* or *"Ask her..."*).
Deliver messages in short, clear chunks (1 to 2 sentences per thought). Never speak in lengthy, uninterrupted technical paragraphs.
Use simple, plain language. Plain English translates with 99%+ accuracy. Never use idioms (*"ballpark figure"*), slang, or bureaucratic acronyms.
Use qualified professional translators or accredited on-demand LSP interpreters. NEVER use minor children or family members to interpret legal or medical matters.
Use the Teach-Back Method: *"To ensure I was clear, could you tell me what our next steps are?"* Never assume a simple *"Yes"* means understanding (it often reflects politeness or shame).
